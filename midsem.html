Mid-Semester Scope

What is being presented at the mid-term review, how much of the project it covers, and the reasoning behind where the line was drawn.

What the review asks for

The mid-term evaluation allots each group ten minutes of presentation across 10–15 slides, followed by questions. A written report of 10–15 pages is submitted alongside it, in six prescribed sections: Introduction; Literature survey; Problem statement and Methodology; Work done till date, Results and Discussion; Future work; References.

The presentation follows the department's template, which prescribes its own section order: Introduction, Literature Review, Objectives, Methods/Materials, Experimentation/Modeling, Results, Conclusions, Publication, References.

Why the line falls there

The two halves answer different questions

The seven experiments divide naturally. Three establish whether the effect exists and how far it generalises. Four characterise where it breaks down and why. The first group is the finding; the second is its boundary conditions.

A mid-term review is a checkpoint on whether the research question is well posed, the method is sound, and the central result holds. That is exactly the first group. The second group is analysis of failure modes, and failure-mode analysis reads properly only when it is given room — three of those four experiments each need a figure and several paragraphs to be understood rather than merely stated.

Ten minutes will not carry seven experiments

Ten minutes across twelve slides is roughly fifty seconds per slide. Presenting all seven experiments would leave about twenty seconds each, which is enough to display a number and not enough to explain what it means. The choice is between seven results nobody can follow and three results that land, plus an honest account of what else exists.

Nothing is concealed

The four remaining experiments are named on slide 9, each with its headline outcome and its actual figure — the robustness number, the coverage ratio, the impairment that inverts the result, the ablation spread. Any of them can be discussed on request; the numbers are in the report and the underlying results are in this repository. What is deferred is the depth of treatment, not the existence of the result.

The remaining work is stated as a plan

Slide 9 closes with the planned work in priority order, beginning with fibre nonlinearity. That ordering is not arbitrary: the robustness result from Phase 1 already produces a falsifiable prediction about nonlinearity — because nonlinear interference is not Gaussian, and cumulant features rely on Gaussian cancellation at fourth order and above, the feature-based models should degrade further than the CNN. The experiment therefore has a defined outcome either way, which the other two candidate directions do not yet have.

Reproducibility

Every number presented regenerates from a fixed random seed. The dataset is not stored in this repository because it rebuilds byte-for-byte from config.SEED in about five minutes; the full pipeline, from data generation through all seven experiments to final figures, runs in roughly four and a half hours on a laptop CPU with no GPU at any stage.

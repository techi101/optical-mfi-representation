## § 1 — Introduction

Roughly five paragraphs. Establish, in this order:

1. **Optical fibre carries the internet.** Data is sent as light; to send more than
   one bit per pulse the transmitter varies amplitude and phase. Define *symbol*
   and *modulation format* here — the committee may not be optical specialists.
2. **Elastic optical networks change format dynamically.** 64-QAM on a short clean
   link, QPSK on a long noisy one. State the consequence: the receiver cannot
   demodulate until it knows which format arrived.
3. **That is the problem MFI solves** — infer the format from the signal itself,
   with no control channel. Place it inside optical performance monitoring (OPM).
4. **The literature's answer is CNNs on constellation images**, and it works. One
   sentence, no detail — §2 carries that load.
5. **Our question, and why it is not obvious.** The standard comparison changes the
   model *and* the input simultaneously, so it cannot attribute the gain. State
   the contribution in one sentence: we add an MLP on the same 15 features to
   separate the two. Close with the headline: representation is worth ~3× the
   algorithm.

Include **Fig. `sample_constellations.png`** here, at high and low OSNR. It does
more work than any paragraph you can write.

## § 2 — Literature survey

**Worth real marks. Do not write it as a list of paper summaries.** Organise it
thematically, and make every subsection end with what that line of work leaves
open. Five subsections:

### 2.1 Feature-based MFI — the classical approach
Higher-order cumulants as the standard tool: Swami & Sadler established the
hierarchical cumulant approach, Dobre et al. surveyed the field. Explain *why*
cumulants: Gaussian noise cancels at 4th order and above, so they fingerprint the
constellation robustly. **Gap to state:** they assume a signal model.

### 2.2 Alternative hand-crafted representations
Not everyone uses cumulants. Amplitude histograms (Khan 2016; Wang & Liu), Stokes
space and PCA (Xu et al. 2020; Jiang et al. 2022), ANNs on histogram features with
genetic algorithms (Zhang et al. 2016). **Point to make:** the field has explored
many *representations* but almost always changes the classifier at the same time.
This is where your contribution starts to look inevitable.

### 2.3 Deep learning on constellation images
Wang et al. 2017 (the intelligent constellation analyzer), Huang et al. 2022 (SVM
on constellation images via GLCM — a useful midpoint), Leblebici et al. 2024 (a
recent CNN case study). Khan et al. 2017 for the joint OSNR + MFI variant.
**Gap:** every one of these compares a deep model on images against a shallow
model on features.

### 2.4 The wireless parallel
O'Shea et al. 2016 brought CNNs to raw I/Q in radio. Ge et al. 2021 is the closest
prior work to ours — it analysed representation effects in *wireless* AMC. Tian et
al. 2026 survey the whole DL-AMC field explicitly by data representation.
**Be honest here:** say plainly that the representation question has been asked in
wireless. Our claim is the optical domain, both directions controlled, and a
different conclusion. Examiners respect this; hiding it looks worse.

### 2.5 What nobody has done
Two sentences. Nobody holds the input fixed and varies only the algorithm *and*
holds the algorithm fixed and varies only the input, in the optical case. And
nobody has asked whether the representation affects a model's ability to detect
its own failure (Yang & Sahay 2025 study uncertainty, but for one representation).

## § 4 — Work done till date, Results and Discussion

**The strongest section. Give it the most pages.**

### 4.1 Main comparison
The five-model table with parameter counts. Then the two numbers that matter:
algorithm effect **1.83 pts** (the spread across MLP/SVM/k-NN on identical features),
representation effect **5.32 pts** (the CNN over the **best** of them, the SVM — the
conservative comparison; against the MLP alone it is 5.65). Ratio **2.9×**. Note that
the MLP (deep) landed *below* the SVM (shallow) — depth on features bought nothing.

### 4.2 Accuracy versus OSNR
**Fig. `fig1_accuracy_vs_osnr.png`.** Then restate as the margin, which is what an
optical engineer budgets: CNN reaches 95% at **7 dB**, SVM needs **14 dB**.
Table of ≥95% and ≥99% thresholds.

### 4.3 The representation effect isolated
**Fig. `fig2_representation.png`.** This is the contribution figure.

### 4.4 Generalisation to unseen OSNR
97.45 ± 0.16% on withheld OSNR levels, a drop of 1.27 points. Conclusion: it
learned the physics, not the noise levels. **Fig. `fig9_generalisation.png`.**

### 4.5 Robustness under channel mismatch — **the strongest result**
The 3×2 table (clean/realistic/severe × CNN/SVM). Trained clean, tested severe:
CNN **89.78%**, SVM **21.84%** against a **20%** chance level. Explain the
mechanism: cumulants are statistics of an assumed model; change the impairments
and the boundaries stop meaning anything. **Fig. `fig8_robustness.png`.**

### 4.6 Failure self-detection
At a 90% confidence gate on the severe channel: CNN answers **86.9%** of inputs,
SVM answers **0.2%** — a ratio of **447×**. State that the SVM claims about twice
the accuracy it achieves. Say clearly that this interaction has not, to our
knowledge, been reported before. **Fig. `fig10_confidence.png`.**

### 4.7 Capture length and ablation
CNN with 512 symbols (94.08%) beats SVM with 4096 (93.82%). Ablation: six variants
within 1.26 points across a 9.2× parameter range. **State the honest limit** — the
ablation used one seed, so the supported claim is "capacity is not the binding
constraint", *not* "4 channels is optimal".

### 4.8 Where the SVM wins — the honest negative
I/Q imbalance destroys the cumulants (SVM 20.35% at 2.5 dB gain imbalance while
the CNN holds 95.48%). **But at 150 ps/nm residual chromatic dispersion the SVM
scores 80.14% and the CNN only 54.89%.** Report it prominently.
**Fig. `fig11_tolerance.png`.** Expect this question in the viva — see below.

### 4.9 Verification
Short subsection, high credibility per line. `verify_project.py` reloads saved
weights and recomputes accuracy from scratch (0.986984 reported, 0.986984
recomputed); an always-one-class model scores 20.00% and k-NN on shuffled labels
scores 20.00%. Mention that 135 numbers in the manuscript are generated from the
results JSON and re-derived by an audit script.

## § 5 — Future work

**Nothing exists for this. Write it fresh.** Material to draw on, in descending
order of how good it sounds:

1. **Experimental validation.** Replace simulated captures with measurements from a
   real coherent testbed. Data-request emails are drafted; this is the obvious next
   step and the honest answer to the biggest limitation.
2. **Polarisation-multiplexed signals.** Commercial long-haul sends two independent
   signals on orthogonal polarisations; we model one.
3. **Fibre nonlinearity.** Our channel is linear. Nonlinear noise is not Gaussian,
   so cumulant features would behave differently — and our robustness result
   predicts the feature-based models would suffer more. That is a testable
   prediction, which is a strong thing to put in Future Work.
4. **Joint OSNR estimation.** Deliberately dropped for space; the same image could
   estimate OSNR as well as format.
5. **More formats and other symbol rates.** Accuracy typically falls as classes are
   added; everything here is at 32 GBaud.
6. **Why does dispersion favour the features?** Our own negative result is an open
   question worth naming as future work.
